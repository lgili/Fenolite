## Why

A script can end a track at a pad, but every other point of script copper is a board coordinate: vias, via steps, waypoints, arc points and stitches. The review of 2026-10-05 of the gaps to a complex board (milestone v0.4; `docs/roadmap.md`, "The complex board, c0100–c0120") found two consequences. Both were measured on 2026-10-05, and the code behind them is the same at `origin/dev` `9aba2dff`: `dsl/intents.py` and `dsl/part.py` have not changed, and `_obstacles` of `backends/kicad/copper.py` still makes every pad an obstacle:

- Copper that belongs to a part stays behind when the part moves. Nine vias in a QFN's exposed pad, the part moved 15 mm by `fenolite place --move`, the project rebuilt: the vias stay, the build says only `layout.place-overridden`, and KiCad reports 9 `via_dangling` and 9 unconnected items.
- A stitch cannot make a thermal array in a pad: every pad, of any net, blocks its candidates. A region equal to a 3.1 mm exposed pad keeps 0 of 9.

The review's scale probe, a board of 600 parts generated by a script authored for Fenolite and not committed, hand-computed 549 vias, and could write none for parts the grid placer placed.

## What Changes

- **Anchors.** `part.at(dx, dy)` and `part.pad(n, index=…).at(dx, dy)` name a point in the frame of the part's footprint as its library draws it. They stand wherever script copper takes a point: waypoints, `arc_to`, `via_step(anchor, to=…)`, `design.via(key, anchor, net=…)`, and a stitch's `along`, `region` and `origin`.
- **Resolved after placement.** `resolve_copper` turns each anchor into `at + R(θ)·S·d` (`S` mirrors the bottom side), rounded once, on the placed design. In a build that is the effective placement, so anchored copper follows a part moved in KiCad or by `place` at the next build, with the same uuids. `part_frame` gives the same point to scripts on a model design.
- **Thermal arrays.** `design.stitch(key, net=…, pitch=…, region=part.pad(n))` lays its grid in the pad's frame from its centre, keeps candidates whose via disc plus margin lies inside the pad's copper, and does not count that pad as an obstacle; other pads and every hole still are.

Size: 4.5 design-days.

Limits: anchored copper follows its part at the next build, not at `place`; KiCad re-nets a via of another net inside a pad instead of reporting a short, so only the copper guard of `build` catches an anchor in the wrong pad; a thermal via joined on one layer only gets `via_dangling` until a plane or track of its net reaches it.

## Non-goals

- Fan-out vias generated for the pads of plane nets: c0107.
- Tenting, plugging or filling thermal vias: c0112.
- Zone outlines in a part's frame: nowhere, because zones are edited in place (c0031) and a moving outline would undo KiCad edits. Script keep-outs: c0103.
- A frame per module: no change has it; layout reuse per module is v0.5b in `docs/roadmap.md`.
- `place` moving script copper: nowhere, because `build` regenerates it (c0028).
- Blind or micro thermal vias: nowhere; stitch vias stay through vias and the yardstick needs no HDI.
- Repeated pad numbers in authored footprints: built by c0059.

## Evidence level required

- Where anchors land, and thermal arrays: `KICAD-VERIFIED (9.0.x, 10.0.x)` through the new rows `H-G-FRAME-ANCHOR` (marker vias, which KiCad re-nets to the pad they touch) and `H-K-VIA-IN-PAD`, measured on 2026-10-05.
- Resolution, DSL forms and stitch rules: mechanical (unit scenarios). `copper.EVIDENCE` and `frame.EVIDENCE` keep their levels.
