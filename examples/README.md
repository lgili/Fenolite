# examples

Designs authored for Fenolite (CC0-1.0), used as documentation and as end-to-end tests. Every
example is original work: no file, value, name or layout comes from any organisation's projects.

| example | what it shows |
|---|---|
| `blink_2layer/` | the thin DSL: a mini MCU, a resistor and an LED (bottom side) on a 50 mm × 30 mm two-layer board, from the authored mini library through the folder's own library tables; `fenolite build` turns it into a KiCad 9.0 or 10.0 project: the board, project and rules files, the schematic `blink.kicad_sch` with `sym-lib-table`, and under `lib/` the three footprints and the symbol libraries `Mini.kicad_sym` and `fenolite.kicad_sym` (`docs/schematic.md`); the 29 pins the circuit does not use are marked with `no_connect`, so KiCad's ERC reports nothing on the built project |
| `blink_routed/` | the blink with copper declared by the script: three tracks from pad to pad with waypoints and layer-changing vias, and a row of stitching vias, 11 tracks and 7 vias in all; the build resolves them after placement, so a part moved in KiCad pulls its tracks along (`docs/copper.md`) |
| `board_40parts/` | forty parts in two modules: two mini MCUs, each driving nine LEDs through nine resistors, with an enable pull-up, on a 100 mm × 80 mm two-layer board with a `GND` zone on both layers and one scripted ground via under each controller; only the two controllers are placed (and locked), the rest is left to `fenolite place --strategy grid`; the board minimums come from `design.rules.minimum`, so KiCad's DRC judges the finished board against the script's own rules; it is the second board of the v0.1 acceptance loop (`docs/release/v0.1.md`); its schematic takes one A3 sheet, a row of symbols per module, and KiCad's ERC and parity test report nothing on it |
| `catalog/` | a 5 V to 3.3 V linear supply with an indicator LED from the built-in catalog alone (`Fenolite:` lib ids, no library table); its build is recorded in `tests/data/catalog/golden.json`, the guard of the catalog's move from Python tables to record files (change c0259) |
| `devices/` | parts placed from device records (change c0248, `docs/devices.md`): a 5 V to 3.3 V step-down board whose regulator `U1` comes from `devices/example_buck.toml` beside the script (an illustrative device, not a real product: an authored symbol, a catalog footprint, the map from pin function to pad, ratings and the label `UNVERIFIED`), and whose passives, LED and connector come from the shipped generic `Fenolite:` devices; pins are named by function (`u1.VIN`), and the build reports each device in `result.devices` |
| `sensor_rails/` | interface parameters (change c0252, `docs/dsl.md`, "Rails, grounds and logic"): a 12 V input, the block `buck` whose port `VOUT` promises 3.3 V ± 3 % at 1 A, an MCU and an I2C sensor whose illustrative device records in `devices/` declare their power, I2C and logic ports with operating values and thresholds; whole interfaces are joined with `connect(bus, mcu.i2c1, sen.i2c)`, and the stage `circuit.rules` judges the rails, the levels and the pull-ups. `good.py` passes; `bad.py` adds a 1.1 A fan that overloads `3V3` and pulls the bus up to `V12`, so its build stops with `circuit.rail-current`. Both share `rails/board.py`, and `good.lock` holds the refs |
| `pins/` | pin assignment at the interface level (change c0257, `docs/pins.md`): an MCU whose illustrative record in `devices/` offers its I2C, UART, SPI and ADC on several pin sets, an I2C sensor and a debug header; the script takes `mcu.take(I2C)` and `mcu.take(UART, option="UART2", name="debug")`, and the build chooses the pins, lists them in `result.pins` and records them in the `[pins]` section of the committed `design.lock` |
| `blink_official/` | the same circuit on KiCad's official libraries; DSL source only, built by a `needs_libs` test into a temporary folder |
| `altium_sample/` | the experimental Altium target (`fenolite build … --target altium`): eight parts at the top level and in two modules, a 5 V regulator, a driver with an enable pull-up and an LED, two supplies sharing `GND`, every pin connected by number; its lib ids name `FenoliteSample.SchLib` and `FenoliteSample.PcbLib`, which Fenolite does not ship; the built files are committed under `tests/data/altium/sample/` |
| `altium_kicad/` | the Altium target from KiCad symbols (change c0034): a dual amplifier with common supply pins, a controller with pins on four sides, upright resistors and a connector from the authored CC0 `FenoliteDemo.kicad_sym`, found through the folder's `sym-lib-table`; the build writes their pins and units into `altium_kicad.SchLib`, and the built files are committed under `tests/data/altium/kicad_example/` |
| `altium_hier/` | sheets per module and a signal harness for the Altium target (change c0037, `--altium-sheets modules`): a connector on the top sheet, a controller and a flash memory in two modules, an `SPI` harness between them; `partial.py` adds a harness entry whose net stays on one sheet; the lib ids name `FenoliteHier.SchLib` and `FenoliteHier.PcbLib`, which Fenolite does not ship; the built files are committed under `tests/data/altium/hier/` |
| `altium_hier_board/` | the blink circuit of `blink_2layer/` split into the modules `driver` and `led`, with its board and the same authored libraries, so the Altium build writes a PCB document whose parts link through their sheet symbols |
| `generated/` | footprints generated from package records (change c0260, `docs/footprint-generators.md`): one record of each family in `packages/` beside the script (a 0603 chip, a SOD-123 diode, a SOIC-8, a QFN-16 with exposed pad, a 12-ball grid array, a TO-220, a DIP-8, a 1x2 pin header and a 3-position connector of a series), each with the numbers of a cited public outline and, when the source gives one, the manufacturer's land; `Footprint.from_package` draws them under `drawing/1`, the build writes `lib/Local.pretty/` and the STEP models of the eight footprints with bodies under `lib/Local.3dshapes/`, and `fenolite library generate examples/generated/packages/*.toml --out lib` writes the same files and `lib/Local.PcbLib` without a design |
| `symbols/` | symbols generated from pin tables (change c0261, `docs/symbols.md`): the illustrative regulator `pins/EXB6202.toml` (one unit, an exposed pad stacked on `GND`, one variant) and the illustrative 120-pin controller `pins/EXM120.toml` (split into two signal units and a named power unit, with alternates and one variant); `design.py` generates both with `Symbol.from_pins`, places the regulator through its variant and writes all four symbols into `lib/Example.kicad_sym`; `tests/kicad/library/test_symbol_generate_oracle.py` loads the library in `kicad-cli` |
| `import_python/` | the code `fenolite import` writes from a board authored for Fenolite (`tests/data/import_python/two_sheet`, change c0275; `docs/import.md`): `design.py` with the board, nets, the class `Power`, five parts in two modules with their keys, connections, placements, a zone, a keep-out and drawings; `copper.py` with the copper as `track_table`, `arc_table` and `via_table` rows; the vendored `lib/`, `design.lock` and `PROVENANCE.json`; each `.py` file is marked `Authored for Fenolite` after its provenance header |
| `kit/` | the five sample scripts of the Altium verification kit (`docs/altium-kit.md`): `flat`, `tree`, `routed`, `board6` and `libs`, all on Fenolite's own catalog; `fenolite kit build` builds them into the kit, and nothing it builds is committed |
| `lock/` | the design lock (change c0249): a regulator and an indicator LED in two modules, most parts written without a ref (`reg.part(lib_id=...)` gives the path `reg/U`, `name=` gives `reg/c_in`) and one explicit `J1`; the build assigns the refs per prefix in natural path order and records them in the committed `design.lock`, which a release build checks with `fenolite build ... --frozen`; every symbol and footprint comes from Fenolite's catalog |
| `picks/` | parts chosen by parameters (change c0254, `docs/picking.md`): `design.py` asks an input capacitor by value, rating and dielectric (picked from the illustrative project family `devices/caps.toml`), a feedback divider in the block `buck1` (`voltage_divider`), an RC filter, an LED resistor and the pull-ups of an I2C bus; `divider.py` holds the divider alone; the build picks every part from the shipped `Fenolite:` families and `devices/`, records each pick in the committed `design.lock` and `divider.lock`, and reports what each generator achieved (`gen.achieved`); `fenolite pick examples/picks` lists the picks |
| `families/` | exact part numbers from ordering-code grammars (change c0263, `docs/picking.md`, "Families with ordering codes"): three illustrative `fenolite.family.v1` grammars of an invented manufacturer, `Example Passive`, in `devices/` (`example_mlcc.toml`, value in `eia3`; `example_chip_r.toml`, `eia4` with an opaque tail; `example_film_r.toml`, `rkm`), and `design.py`, whose capacitor and resistor queries the build turns into exact MPNs with their manufacturer, recorded in the committed `design.lock`; `fenolite family decode EX05C471JBT --project examples/families` reads an MPN back |
| `yardstick/` | the yardstick board, at stage 5 of five (`docs/evidence/yardstick.md`): an invented controller of eight identical 48 V buck channels with an isolated high-voltage sense section, 388 parts of KiCad's official libraries and of the generated hole and assembly libraries on a 250 mm × 110 mm six-layer board with a declared stack-up, inner planes, slots under the isolators, plated mounting holes with keep-outs, a high-voltage rule area, the USB pair's rules and impedance target, thermal via arrays, placement rules, a net tie, fiducials, tooling holes and test points; one function makes a channel and is called eight times, references are `n·100 + k`, the script places every part and draws no track, and every value is stated once at the top as `chosen for the example` or with a source id; DSL source only, built for KiCad 10 from the verified library cache by a `needs_libs` test and taken through the loop every night by `tools/yardstick.py run` (routed by Freerouting, judged by KiCad), with a time and memory budget per step |
