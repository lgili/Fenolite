# Changelog

All notable changes to Fenolite are documented here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and the project uses
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) (pre-1.0: minor versions may break).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Value ranges** (c0246): `Range` values with exact bounds built at the DSL boundary: `q.pct(p)` and `q.pct(lo, hi)`, `q.max()`, `q.min()` and `between(a, b)`; worst-case `+ - * /` over a fixed table of unit products and quotients (no solver), `within()` and `text()`. A part given a range keeps its value text (the nominal) and records the range in the new optional model field `Component.value_range` as `ValueRange` bounds of canonical exact decimals (`Decimal10`, up to 15 digits and an exponent of ±30, rounded outward). KiCad and Altium outputs do not change.
- **Connection helpers** (c0247): `series(net, part, ..., net)` connects a two-terminal chain, naming the joints `<left>-<right>`; `decouple(a, b, *caps, near=, within=)` also records a `near()` rule per capacitor; `pull_up` and `pull_down` take a net or an `I2C` bus; `part.a`/`part.b` are the ends of a two-pin part; `part.unused_no_connect(keep=())` marks at build every open pin except `power_in` and must-connect pins. New build errors `build.not-two-terminal` and `build.must-connect-unconnected`.
- **Design lock** (c0249): a part can be created without a ref (`design.part(...)`, `module.part(...)`, `name=`, `prefix=`); the build assigns the lowest free number per prefix in a natural path order and records it in `<script stem>.lock` (schema `fenolite.lock.v0`, sections `[refs]`, and `[picks]`/`[pins]` reserved), written through the build's plan and confirm. `fenolite build --lock` and `--frozen` (exit 7, `FEN-7004`, when the lock would change); `design.moved()` carries entries to the new path; `lock.*` issue codes. Designs with explicit refs only get no lock and build as before.
- **Net roles, phase 1: role copper** (c0243): `fenolite copper PATH` (KiCad board or built project, through the reviewed-plan protocol, with `--gate drc`) and the DSL call `design.role_copper(...)` (resolved by the build after placement, for the KiCad and the Altium target) generate the copper of the confirmed and declared net roles (c0222) of a placed board. Each `power` net gets one locked zone per layer, the union of bands along a rectilinear tree between its pads, hot-loop legs first, sized from the role's current by the S-0851 rule of thumb or, with a temperature rise, the S-0269 capacity fit, and widened up to twice that where the clearance allows; a `switch_node` keeps exactly its required width and the switching distance from its victims (`role.sw.*`); ground gets a plane per reference layer with stitching vias (at least two per overlap, 5 mm pitch by default); pads connect solid for power, switch-node and high-voltage nets and by thermal relief on through-hole ground pads only; via arrays where a net changes layer are counted from its current (`milliamps_per_via`); each `high_voltage` net gets a clearance rule and a keep-out band at the user's own distance (`roles.limit-missing` without one). Phase 2's locked routes and `role.corridor.<group>.<net>` areas are obstacles; run first, phase 1 writes provisional corridors of the same names, which `route --roles critical` replaces. The result is judged by c0222's role judge on the simulated pour (`roles.copper-worse`, nothing written); a manifest of fingerprints in `.fenolite/role-copper.json` keeps items the user edited (`roles.copper-kept`, `--regenerate`) and makes a second run plan nothing. The Altium build now resolves generated and role copper in its in-memory KiCad build (zones as polygons poured by c0215, keep-outs, Polygon Connect Style rules); `kicad.generate.altium` remains only for `--copper-from`. Each reference board of `examples/roles/` gains `good_roles.py` and `bad_roles.py`; eight issue codes (`roles.copper-*`, `roles.corridor-blocked`), hypotheses `H-G-ROLE-COPPER-*` and sources S-0860 to S-0863.
- **Net roles, phase 2: critical routes** (c0244): `fenolite route PATH --router octilinear --roles critical [--group NAME]... [--rip] [--gate drc]` routes the gate loops and Kelvin pairs of the confirmed or declared net roles (c0222) before anything else, through the reviewed-plan protocol. c0228's octilinear router gains a role mode (feature `roles`, `RoutingJob.roles`, one `RoleOutcome` per route): gate loops first, then Kelvin pairs; a Kelvin branch enters its sense pad on the inner half, a gate return starts at the source or Kelvin-source pad, branches touch their own power copper only at their end pads, keep the profile's distance from switching copper, take the smallest gate-loop area or the shortest Kelvin pair, and the pattern-routing Z shapes join the five shapes. A `role_check` extra judges the roles before and after the job and removes a group that gets worse (`route.role-refused`); every written item is locked and every branch gets a corridor `role.corridor.<group>.<net>` that pours fill around. `result.roles` gives each group's status, findings before and after and its copper; a group that cannot be routed gets `route.role-unrouted` with a reason and a proposal from closed sets, and unconfirmed inferred groups are listed and not routed (`route.role-unconfirmed`).
- **Boards to code** (c0275): `fenolite import PATH --to python --out DIR` turns a KiCad project, board or schematic into a Fenolite project folder through the reviewed-plan protocol: `design.py` (board and cut-outs, nets with their source names, net classes and minimums, parts with explicit refs and c0249 keys in the modules of their `fenolite.path`, connections, placements with the source lock flags, zones, rule areas and drawings, in the frame of `place()` so the build writes the source coordinates exactly), `copper.py` (tracks, arcs and vias as tables), the vendored `lib/` (footprints brought back from the board through the library derive, variants as `<name>_2`, symbols drawn from their pads) with project tables, `design.lock`, `PROVENANCE.json` (`fenolite.import-provenance.v0`: inputs with SHA-256, upgrades, tools, findings, self-check) and copies of `--licence-file`; every `.py` file starts with the SPDX, copyright and `Converted from …` lines (`--licence`, `--copyright`, `--source`, `--revision`). Before anything is planned the code is built in a private folder and compared with the source at equivalence levels 1 to 5 (new profile `import`) and copper item by item (`import.unequal`, exit 5). An item the DSL cannot express refuses the import (exit 7, `FEN-7001`) unless `--allow-lossy`, each kind reported (`import.item-unexpressed`; the kinds of c0277 stay lossy until it is merged). A board below the read floor is upgraded by `kicad-cli pcb upgrade` on a private copy (`--no-upgrade` refuses it). Ten `import.*` codes, result schema `fenolite.import.v0`, user guide `docs/import.md`, the authored example `examples/import_python/` and a residue test for converted code. The DSL gains `Design.track_table`, `arc_table` and `via_table` (rows of integer nanometres; row `i` keeps the copper uuid of item `i` of its key) and `key=` for unnamed zones and rule areas; `fenolite.library.derive_from_model` derives one library per nickname from a board read once. Adoption (`--adopt`), Altium inputs and sheet modules follow. The agent guide's generated pages may now hold 800 lines.

## [0.6.0] - 2026-10-29

### Added

- **Net roles, phase 0** (c0222): Fenolite infers what each net of a power board does (`power`, `switch_node`, `ground`, `kelvin`, `gate`, `signal`, `high_voltage`) and its hot loops, gate loops and Kelvin pairs from the circuit, with a part class, a rule and a confidence for each, and judges the copper against them through the analyses of `dev` (`loop_areas`, `analyze_power`, `path_network`, `analyze_necks`, `analyze_split`, `analyze_distances`, `analyze_returns`, `analyze_coupling`, `analyze_ties`), defining no area, section, capacity or gap of its own. `fenolite roles PATH [--check]` shows and checks them, read-only until `--accept`, `--accept-all [--min-confidence]`, `--set NET=KIND[:KEY=N]`, `--reject`, `--accept-group`, `--reject-group` or `--current NET=AMPS` plans the role document `<board stem>.roles.json` (schema `fenolite.roles.v0`) or `.fenolite/rules.json` of a built project; `--check` takes the measuring options of `analyze` and runs no tool. The stage `roles.rules` of `fenolite check` (both pipelines, after `placement.rules`) runs the same judge on a board with stored or declared roles and is skipped with `no-roles` otherwise. A design script declares roles with `design.net_role`, `hot_loop`, `kelvin`, `gate_loop`, `role_limits` and `part_class`, stored as `declared` in `RuleSet.roles`; a rebuild keeps the confirmed ones. Seventeen `roles.*` codes (with `fenolite explain`) and the check code `check.roles-unreadable`; a finding against a default of the role profile (`fenolite.roles.profile`, each default with its source, `docs/roles.md`) is at most a warning, an error only against your limit on a confirmed or declared role. Three reference boards authored for Fenolite (`examples/roles/buck_shunt`, `half_bridge`, `bms`, good and bad) with CC0 parts in `tests/data/libs/Roles.*`; the good variants pass KiCad DRC and give no warning, the bad ones exactly their designed findings, on the KiCad and the Altium build. Six `H-G-ROLE-` hypotheses registered. The agent guide's generated pages may now hold 750 lines.
- **Release 0.6.0: the MCP server and the board engineering tools** (c0242): `fenolite mcp` and `fenolite edit` (c0195, c0196); the board engineering tools of c0175 in their own changes: router input fidelity, ground ties, barrier crossings, return paths, practice checks, pour shapes, region placement, layout reuse and group moves, copper tidy, DRC trials and `--gate drc`, diff gates, region renders, designators and silkscreen clipping, test-point placement, the octilinear router, generated copper, library fidelity, file safety and fabrication read-back (c0197 to c0214, c0223 to c0229); the Python API (c0206); computed Altium pours, experimental (c0215); the release from a tag (c0221); reliability, viewer review, the catalog browser and the public presentation (c0232 to c0240); and the hypotheses and kit Part J of the Altium edit sessions (c0241). The record `docs/release/v0.6.md` gives, change by change, what ships, its proof and its open tasks; the maintainer's tests (the Altium kit runs, the viewer reviews, a KiCad GUI save, the runs of a real agent) are deferred to v0.7, and the v1.0 freeze (c0216 to c0220) is held by his decision of 2026-10-29. Every Altium write kind stays experimental.
- **Altium edit sessions, measured first** (c0241, task 1.1; package 12 of c0175, moved on 2026-10-29): the hypotheses of the edit session (`H-A-EDIT-*`, fourteen rows: what Altium Designer changes in a document when it moves, turns, flips, deletes, copies, places, re-layers or re-rules one object, and what a plain save rewrites) and of the integrity rules (`H-A-INTEG-*`, seven rows: polygon sub-indices, pour indices, unique ids, parameter groups and orphans, the model store, two layer fields) are registered, each `INFERRED` with its kit case, before any write code. Kit Part J (`docs/evidence/altium-edit.md`): phase A, an authored two-layer board (`tests/data/altium_edit/edit.py`) and cases J1 to J22 that the maintainer edits once each and saves in Altium Designer, built outside the repository by `FENOLITE_ALTIUM_PART_J=<folder> uv run pytest tests/unit/backends/altium/test_part_j.py -k phase_a` with `expected.md`, the steps in Brazilian Portuguese and `SHA256SUMS`; phase B (J23 to J33) is described for the documents the session will write and for planted faults. No edit is written yet.
- **Public presentation (c0237):** a workflow-oriented README, first-board tutorial and documentation index; actual viewer screenshots, cross-probing animation, social preview and reviewed launch material. Package metadata describes shipped KiCad/Altium features; experimental limits remain visible.
- **Designators placed on the silkscreen** (c0212): `fenolite silk BOARD --designators [--keep] [--refs GLOB] [--sizes LENGTHS] [--angles 0,90] [--clearance LENGTH] [--gap LENGTH] [--measure auto|kicad|estimate] [--drc]` puts the reference of every part beside its courtyard, clear of pad copper, holes, silkscreen, other texts, other parts' courtyards and the board edge, at 0° or 90°, mirrored on the bottom; a row or a column of equal parts shares one place, so their texts line up. The ink of each text is measured with `kicad-cli` (one scratch board, cached per tool version, `fenolite.silk.extents`, `backends.kicad.textink`), or estimated from a glyph table that `tools/measure_glyphs.py` measured (`silk.estimated`). Every written designator is checked again (`silk.check-failed`), `--drc` has KiCad judge the board before and after (`KICAD-VERIFIED`; `H-K-SILK-DRC`, `H-K-SILK-INK`, `H-K-SILK-ESTIMATE`), and a part without room keeps its text (`silk.no-place`). Facts in `docs/formats/kicad/text.md`, user guide `docs/assembly.md`, "Designators". The generated page `commands` of the agent guide may now hold 700 lines, and the skill folder 300 000 bytes. Silkscreen clipping (c0226) and test-point placement (c0229) followed as changes of their own.
- **Silkscreen clipped at the mask openings** (c0226): `fenolite silk BOARD --clip [--margin LENGTH]` cuts every silkscreen line, arc, circle and rectangle or polygon outline of the board and of its footprints where its ink comes near a pad's mask opening (its copper grown by its mask expansion) or a pad's hole, keeping the margin (default: the project's `silk_clearance` rule, else 0) and writing the rest as lines and arcs; filled shapes and texts are not cut (`silk.clip-skipped`). Each piece is checked again (`silk.check-failed`), and `--drc` has KiCad judge the board (`H-K-SILK-CLIP`). A footprint's own outline is cut alike on every instance, so `fenolite library --out` makes one footprint of them; each changed footprint is named (`silk.footprint-changed`) with the `fenolite library` line in `result.library`, and a cut at another part's pads is `silk.cut-by-other`. `--clip` and `--designators` run together, the clip first. Measured first: KiCad's `silk_over_copper` measures to the pad's copper, only for pads with a mask opening, with the limit of a `silk_clearance` rule and not `min_silk_clearance` (`H-K-SILK-MASK`; `docs/formats/kicad/drc.md`, "Silkscreen over pads"). User guide `docs/assembly.md`, "Clipping".
- **Test points placed** (c0229): `fenolite testpoints BOARD --place --side top|bottom [--grid LENGTH] [--reach LENGTH] [--size LENGTH] [--nets GLOB]... [--drc]` adds a probe of `design.test_point` and one straight track for each net the side does not reach, on the nearest grid site near the net's copper that keeps the clearance from other copper, holes and earlier probes, the edge clearance, and stays off courtyards, silkscreen and forbidding rule areas, the probes `--min-pitch` apart. Fenolite's copper check judges the board before it is written (`testpoint.check-failed`); `--drc` has KiCad judge it (`testpoint.drc-new`, `H-K-TP-PLACE`; the library-table type `lib_footprint_issues` aside). The probes are board-only footprints, so a rebuild keeps them and their tracks (`testpoint.board-only`); a net without room is `testpoint.no-site`. `result.place` holds the coverage before and after. `fenolite.silk.board.silkscreen_ink` gives a side's silkscreen as shapes. User guide `docs/assembly.md`, "Placing test points".
- **Group moves, pads paired by net, and zones of a reused block** (c0227): `fenolite place --turn REFS --about X,Y --angle DEGREES` and `--shift REFS --by DX,DY` (the strategy `group`) move a module and its satellites as one rigid piece: each part's angle comes from the move composed with its placement, so bottom parts turn right, and the tracks, vias and zones that join only the group's pads move with it. Every moved pad is measured and the copper check compares the board before and after (`place.group-failed`, error; `place.fill-stale`, info). `--pose-search --within LENGTH` ranks the four turns and a grid of shifts by the demand across the group's border, without writing. `place --strategy reuse` now pairs pads that share a number (three `GND` pads) by the nets of their pads (`result.reuse.parts[].by_net`), and with `--copper` copies a zone of the block whose fill joins paired pads only, with its fill, rebuilding the outline of a zone read without one from its fill (`zone-beyond-block`, `zone-unfilled`, `pad-lost`). Evidence `INFERRED` (`H-G-GROUP-MOVE`, `H-G-REUSE-ZONE`, `H-K-GROUP-DRC`); `docs/placement.md`, "Group moves".
- **Octilinear router** (c0228): `fenolite route BOARD --router octilinear` is a built-in router of short connections, such as gate loops and sense pairs, with no tool and no via. For each open connection (a minimum spanning tree over the pads of a net, pads joined by its copper counting as one) it tries a straight segment, 45° then straight, straight then 45° and the two L shapes on every allowed layer, and keeps the first that keeps the clearance in force from other nets' copper and holes and the edge clearance and makes no acute joint. `--router-option max-length=LENGTH` (20 mm) leaves longer connections open (`route.unrouted`); `--router-option rounds=N` (3) routes the failed connections first again. A differential pair of two pads per net is routed as two tracks at the pair gap (`route.pair-not-routed` otherwise). `route` now gives every router the clearance in force and the copper check of the project's rules in the job's `extra`, and the router removes a connection the check refuses (`route.check-refused`). Router feature `short` in `fenolite capabilities`. Evidence `INFERRED` (`H-G-OCTI-CHECK`, `H-K-OCTI-DRC`; `KICAD-VERIFIED` with `--gate drc`); `docs/routing.md`, "octilinear".
- **Pour shapes and the split of the current** (c0213): `fenolite analyze` gains three opt-in kinds on the raster of the zones in their copper states. `necks` finds the places where a pour narrows below the `min_width_nm` of a `[[neck]]` row (a neck between wider copper, a long thin tip, a sliver), each an `analysis.neck-narrow` error. `loops` measures the area of the closed path through the pads of each `[[loop]]` row, along copper where the pads share a net, judged by `max_area_nm2` (`analysis.loop-area`), and `--loop-baseline FILE` gives its change against another board. `balance` gives the copper at each pad of every two-pad SMD part and their ratio, for tombstoning. `--kinds power --split` solves each power path on the raster and gives every track, fill and via its share of the current, with the resistance checked against the interval of `power`.

- **Practice checks** (c0204): `fenolite analyze` gains six opt-in kinds. `connectivity` gives the groups of pads of each net with every zone in a copper state, `saved_fill`, `simulated` or `outline`: a zone whose fill is missing, or stale (copper where a later keep-out, cut-out, outline or another net's copper forbids it), is poured again on a raster (`--pour-grid`, 0.1 mm by default), and a net that falls apart is `analysis.net-split`. `dead` finds fill islands joined to no pad (`analysis.copper-dead`) and, with `--dead-distance`, copper far from its pads (`analysis.copper-far`). `decoupling` gives the nearest capacitor of each power-input pin along copper and the loop area, judged by a `[[decoupling]]` row (`analysis.decoupling-far`). `bodies` finds copper of a class under the bodies or courtyards of parts named by a `[[body_copper]]` row (`analysis.body-copper`). `relief` measures the spokes of each thermal relief against a `[[current]]` row (`analysis.relief-exceeded`). `coupling --aggressor GLOB --victim GLOB` ranks pairs by `Σ ℓ / (1 + (s/h)²)` with their broadside overlap and each victim's reference coverage; the score ranks pairs and is not a voltage.
- **Initial placement by regions** (c0205): `fenolite place --strategy regions` puts each domain of parts in its own region of the board, packed by the grid: the islands of nets once the `--barrier` parts are removed, or `--group NAME=GLOB[,GLOB...]`, in one band of the outline each (`--split x|y`) or in `--region NAME=X1,Y1,X2,Y2`. Each barrier part (isolator, transformer) is centred across the boundary between the two regions it joins. `result.regions` reports per region the parts placed and not placed and its fill, and the open connections that cross from one region to another; new warnings `place.region-unassigned`, `place.region-barrier` and `place.region-crossing`; evidence `INFERRED` (`H-G-REGION-PLACE`).
- **A Python API for every command** (c0206): `fenolite.api.run(command, *args, cwd=None, seed=None, timestamp=None)` runs any command in the process, through the dispatcher of `fenolite`, and returns a `Reply` with the exit code, the envelope and the error object the command line prints with `--json`, without a subprocess, without printing and without changing the process's working folder; the mutation protocol is unchanged, and a plan id is shared with the command line. Typed calls return records: `read_design`, `read_board` (`Board`), `connectivity` (`ConnectivityReport`), `analyze_current` and `analyze_distances` (`AnalysisReport`). `docs/api.md` lists every public name of `fenolite.api`, frozen for 1.0.
- **Gated and three-way differences** (c0207): `fenolite diff A B --allow track,via` makes each change of another kind a `diff.not-allowed` error (exit 5) naming its path. `fenolite diff BASE OURS --three-way THEIRS --out FILE` plans `THEIRS` with the board items (keyed by uuid) that changed from `BASE` to `OURS` applied, keeping every other byte of `THEIRS`, under the mutation protocol; an item `THEIRS` changed too is `diff.conflict` and nothing is written, and boards of two versions are `diff.unmergeable`. KiCad boards only. The records view of two Altium files counts records that differ only in the spelling of a value (`10mil` and `10.0mil`, `T` and `TRUE`) in `result.resave` and leaves them out of `differences`. `diff` is now a mutating command; without `--three-way` it plans no write.
- **Copper tidy** (c0208): `fenolite tidy BOARD [--prune] [--chamfer] [--leg LENGTH] [--nets GLOB] [--drc]` removes dangling tracks, arcs and vias, again and again until nothing more goes, and turns right-angle joints of two tracks into 45° corners. Each edit stays only when its net keeps its connected groups and the copper check, with the project's rules, finds nothing new; locked and script copper stay. `--drc` runs `kicad-cli` DRC on both boards and writes nothing when a violation type grows (`KICAD-VERIFIED` otherwise; `H-K-TIDY-DRC`). Package `fenolite.tidy`, seven `tidy.*` codes, `docs/copper.md` "Copper tidy". Straightening runs, the style report and the octilinear router stay with c0175.
- **KiCad DRC exclusions written** (c0209): `fenolite check BOARD --exclude CODE:ITEM_A:ITEM_B --reason TEXT --confirm` stores one exclusion in the board's `.kicad_pro`, in both item orders, with the reason as its comment. The finding is named by its code and the items of its `where` (or KiCad uuids); the marker position the key needs is found by probe keys on a copy, and a last `kicad-cli` run confirms the key before the write is planned (`check.exclusion-unmatched`, `-ambiguous`, `-unlocated`, `-present`). `check` judges the two keys as one exception and lists it in `summary.accepted` of `drc.kicad`.
- **Trial edits gated by DRC** (c0209): `fenolite.api.trial(board, edit, gate=False)` applies an edit to a copy, refills the zones (KiCad 10), runs `kicad-cli` DRC on the edited and the unedited copy, and maps each new finding to the ids of the edit's items; with `gate=True` it drops the items that bring new findings until none is left and returns the board to write. A `--gate drc` option of the writing commands stays in c0175.
- **Layout reuse** (c0210): `fenolite place BOARD --from REFERENCE --only U1,C1,C2` (`--strategy reuse`) copies the placement of a block of a reference board (any board a backend reads) onto parts of a KiCad board by a rigid fit over their pads: a quarter turn, a mirror and a translation by least squares, on the pads whose number occurs once in both footprints, first of the anchor (`--anchor`, which stays), then of each part, whose angle and side come from the fit, so a reference read with the Y axis up is copied correctly. `--map TARGET=REFERENCE` copies a second channel, `--tolerance` bounds each pad's distance (`reuse.no-fit` beyond it, `reuse.mirror-suspected` for a part that fits only mirrored). Moved pads are measured against their goals and the copper check runs before and after the moves. `--copper` copies the tracks, arcs and vias joined to the block's pads onto the matching nets, prunes what ends nowhere, drops islands and copies that the copper check flags, and locks the rest; `--drc` compares `kicad-cli` DRC before and after. `result.reuse` holds the transform, the fit per part and the copied and dropped copper. Package `fenolite.reuse`, eight `reuse.*` codes, `docs/placement.md` "Layout reuse"; evidence `INFERRED` (`H-G-REUSE-FIT`, `H-K-REUSE-DRC`). Pads paired by their nets, copied zones and group moves stay with c0175.
- **Region renders** (c0211): `fenolite render BOARD --region X1,Y1,X2,Y2 -o DIR` writes `DIR/region.svg`, one rectangle of the board drawn by Fenolite from the model without `kicad-cli`: pads, tracks, arcs, vias, holes, slots, the board edge and the zone copper in its state (saved fill, simulated pour drawn as its cells, or outline), on a grid labelled in board millimetres (`--grid LENGTH`). `--nets` labels each pad with its net, `--open` draws the open connections as straight lines, and `--cut X1,Y1,X2,Y2` (with `--cut-width`, `--cut-clearance`) draws a barrier cut and its ways per layer from the crossing analysis. Every element carries `class` and `data-*` attributes and exact millimetre coordinates; `result.region` holds the counts, the copper states, the zones and the open connections drawn. New codes `render.region-empty` (warning) and `render.copper-simulated` (info); evidence `INFERRED` (`H-G-REGION-RENDER`). `fenolite.analysis.connectivity.connectivity` takes `pour=` to find the open connections on the copper of the practice checks.
- **Copper tidy straightens, enters T-joints and reports the style** (c0214): `fenolite tidy BOARD --straighten` redraws each run of tracks between fixed points (pads, vias, joints of three) as a shape of at most three segments at 0°, 45° and 90° with fewer weighted turns (45° weighs 1, 90° weighs 3, never acute, free inside the run's own pad), kept only when the net keeps its groups and the copper check finds nothing new; nets of a differential pair or with a length rule stay (`tidy.straighten-skipped`). `--chamfer` also gives a T-joint a 45° entry, and `--zones` cuts the convex right-angle corners of zone outlines, keeping every item the zone touched. With no step option, prune, straighten and chamfer run in that order. `fenolite tidy BOARD --report [--compare REFERENCE]` writes nothing and counts, per net class and layer, the joints at 45°, 90° and acute (`tidy.acute`), the jogs, the widths and the vias (`H-G-TIDY-STYLE`); a run of the steps gives `result.style` before and after. `H-K-TIDY-DRC` now covers the three steps. Tasks 7.2 to 7.5 of c0175; the octilinear router stays there.
- **Library fidelity** (c0223): pads converted from Altium to KiCad get their paste and mask openings by their kind (a surface pad its side's paste and mask, a through-hole pad both masks) and their margins, from a manual expansion or the `All` rule; a pad that a scoped rule may govern is a `pad-margin` loss, but a rule whose scope selects no pad (`IsVia`, `IsTrack`) is left out, so the public documents give none. `Pad.mask_margin` and `Pad.paste_margin` hold them, and the KiCad writers write them as `solder_mask_margin` and `solder_paste_margin`. Every pad takes the pin's net on the board; the generated KiCad schematic holds one hidden pin per further pad, so KiCad's netlist, ERC and parity test see them; `netlist.assignment_compare` and level 2 of `equivalent` name every pad. `Component.pin_pad_map` holds such a pin in several pairs, and `Component.pads_of(pin)` reads it. A design whose pins have one pad each is written byte for byte as before. **The model version stays `"0"`, at a cost: Fenolite 0.2.x and earlier read a document that holds a pin of several pads without an error and apply one pad per pin.** New finding `model.pin-pad-map` (error) for a pad that two pins name; a build of a script with such a pad keeps its own error beside it (`altium.pin-pad-map-invalid`, `build.pin-pad-map-invalid`).
- An Altium build bonds a pin to every pad its `pad_map` lists (c0123). The release 0.2.1 fixed the build for a map of one pad per pin; now each pad of a pin carries the pin's net in the PCB document, script copper and `--copper-from` are checked against every pad, and the map record of the pin in the schematic and its library lists all its pads. RT-A2 compares the map. `fenolite check`, the parity test and `equivalent` know the map of a built Altium project from 0.3.0: they report nothing for the mapped pins, where 0.2.1 reports `netlist.assignment-differs`. A map that names a missing pin or pad, or that leaves one pad to two pins, is refused with `altium.pin-pad-map-invalid`, as in 0.2.1. **The files of an Altium build change only for a script whose `pad_map` gives a pin several pads.**
- The Altium import names the components of a sheet that several sheet symbols name per channel: by the project's PCB document, else by the project's designator format (`D9_1` … `D9_12` instead of `D9` twelve times). On the public project with such a sheet, the schematic and the board now share 694 pad assignments instead of 508. The import instantiates repeated sheets: a sheet symbol `Repeat(NAME, first, last)` gives one channel per index, each with its own components, nets and designators (`$ChannelIndex` and `$ChannelAlpha` are resolved; a sheet entry `Repeat(NAME)` takes one member of the parent's bus per channel; a net inside a channel is named per channel); `altium.import.repeated-sheet` is now only the statement that cannot be instantiated. Imported components carry the pin-to-pad map of their footprint model (`pin_pad_map`), which `check` and level 2 of `equivalent` apply; `altium.import.pin-map` (info) counts the map records that one pad per pin cannot say. The `Repeat` part rests on Altium's documentation and authored sheets (no public file holds one), and a board links to such channels by designator only; the annotation file is still not read (c0083).
- The Altium PCB document holds stacks of up to 16 signal layers, blind and buried vias, board texts, graphics, keep-outs and non-plated holes; polygons are written unpoured and repoured in Altium. `result.pcb` of an Altium build counts every model item as written or not lowered, and an item without a record is named by `altium.not-lowered` (`where` = `<kind>/<id>`). Changed: a blind or buried via is written instead of refused; `altium.via-unsupported` is a warning for micro vias only, and the build goes on without them; a board's keep-outs, texts, graphics and holes are written instead of reported per kind; a copper count that is odd or above 32 gives the stack of the two outer layers and `altium.not-lowered` (`stackup`) instead of `altium.copper-stack`. A keep-out holds its restrictions in the key Altium saves and in the key KiCad's importer reads, so a KiCad import carries them. Component bodies are not written yet (reported one by one), and the model's board holes have no slots (c0085).
- `equivalent --level 5` compares routing per net: which pads the copper joins, the vias per layer pair and the routed length per layer. Differences are located at the net (`equiv.route-missing`, `equiv.route-connectivity`, `equiv.route-vias`, `equiv.route-length`); copper that reaches no pad and nets that depend on a zone without a fill are reported and fail nothing (`equiv.route-stub`, `equiv.route-unjudged`, under `result.notices`). `--tolerance-ppm` and a profile's `tolerance_ppm` set a relative tolerance for routed lengths. **Behaviour change:** without `--level`, `equivalent` now runs level 5 when both sides hold a track, an arc or a via, so two routed boards that differ only in their routing exit 5 where they exited 0; pass `--level 4` for the earlier result. The triangle holds at level 5 on `kicad-cli` 10.0.6 for the routed sample and the seven public PCB documents (c0089).
- An Altium build writes an output job set up from the export preset and draws the script's drawing sheet on its schematics; `template build --target altium` writes a `.SchDot` from a sheet specification. The job (`<name>.OutJob`, on by default beside a PCB document, `--altium-outjob off` to leave it out) holds Gerber, NC drill, pick-and-place, bill-of-materials, schematic-print and PCB-print outputs with their source documents and two containers, and no output setting: the options of `--altium-outjob-preset` are listed in `result.outjob.defaults` to be set in Altium. The drawing sheet of `design.sheet(drawing_sheet=…)` is drawn on every Altium schematic as lines and texts on a custom sheet of the paper's size, with the title block as sheet parameters; a logo and a text that mixes a token with other text are losses that need `--allow-lossy`. Both files are proved by Fenolite's own readers only and stay `INFERRED` until Altium has opened them (c0087).
- An Altium build writes the script's rules by kind and scope, names every rule it cannot lower with its reason, and `export --altium-rul` writes them as a rule file. Seven Altium rule kinds carry the neutral ones (Clearance, Width, Routing Via Style, Hole Size, Board Outline Clearance, Hole To Hole Clearance, Minimum Annular Ring), scoped to all objects, a net, a net class or their conjunction; a rule is written exactly or not at all. **Changed:** the single `altium.not-lowered` info with `where` `design-rules` is gone; each rule that is not written gives one `altium.not-lowered` warning with `where` `design-rules/<kind>`, and `result.rules` lists what was written and what was not. A `clearance` or `edge_clearance` minimum of the script now reaches the PCB document and precedes the class rules; a width, via or hole-size minimum is reported, because Altium's record also holds a maximum. The import maps Board Outline Clearance, Hole To Hole Clearance and Minimum Annular Ring (c0084; that Altium applies the rules is not yet confirmed).
- `fenolite kit` builds the Altium verification kit, checks the files a run leaves and records the run: the base of the label `ALTIUM-VERIFIED(kit)`. `kit build` writes five sample projects from the scripts under `examples/kit/`, a checklist (`STEPS.md`), a result form and a script that a user may start inside Altium for the compile steps; the kit is the same byte for byte on every machine. `kit verify` reads the documents that Altium saved with Fenolite's readers, compares them with the kit's own at the levels of `equivalent`, checks the typed values and lists strings that look like a home folder or a login name. `kit record` writes the archive of the results and a run record without any path or user name; `kit status` lists the runs and the rows whose run is stale. No run was made yet, so no row carries the label; the copper check of a re-saved board waits for c0088 and is reported as pending (`docs/altium-kit.md`, c0091).
- A model with a board can be written as Altium documents; RT-A2 compares footprints and copper, and the new level RT-A3 measures an import, write and re-import over the public corpus. `AltiumBackend().write(design)` (and `fenolite.lens.altium.write_model` for a design read from a KiCad board, whose rounded pads need KiCad's corner ratio) writes the PCB document from the model alone and a schematic generated from the circuit; what the writers do not carry is counted per kind and reported as `altium.not-lowered`, and a loss of copper, pads or footprints is refused (`FEN-7001`) without `allow_lossy`. The write is experimental and is not in the capability report. An Altium build now stores the board it wrote in `.fenolite/board.json` (footprints with their pads, tracks, arcs, vias, zones), so `roundtrip.rta2` compares every kind of the written scope and no longer has `summary.not_in_model`; a project built earlier is skipped with `model-predates-board` until it is built again. No project file of a build changes. New: the opt-in check stage `roundtrip.rta3` with the codes `check.rta3-failed` and `check.rta3-unwritten`, and `fenolite roundtrip PATH --level rta0|rta1|rta2|rta3` for Altium input. On the seven public PCB documents that are not heavy the two imports are equal inside the written scope and KiCad's importer reads each rewrite as Fenolite does; a rewrite is not a copy: footprint graphics, bodies, shapes on copper and poured fills are not in it, and `docs/evidence/altium-roundtrip.md` gives the counts (c0090).
- A footprint of the model holds its graphics and free texts, and a rounded pad its corner ratio (c0126): `FootprintInstance.graphics`, `FootprintInstance.texts` (in the pad frame) and `Pad.corner_ratio` (parts per million of the pad's shorter side; `Design.validate()` reports `model.corner-ratio`). `fenolite diff` compares them as the kinds `footprint_graphic` and `footprint_text`. **Changed: the Altium import maps the tracks, arcs, fills, regions and texts of a component to its footprint (the designator and the comment become the fields `Reference` and `Value`), so `altium.import.unmapped` no longer lists `footprint-graphics` on a normal document and an imported `board.json` is larger** (the own samples grow by 41 % to 76 %; measured on the public corpus: owed). **Changed: a rewrite of an Altium board (`AltiumBackend().write`, RT-A3) keeps the lines, arcs, fills, regions, designator and comment of its footprints on the overlays and on Mechanical 1 to 16**; a graphic on copper is counted as `footprint-copper`, a loss that needs `allow_lossy`. `fenolite build --target altium` places the footprints into the model and goes through the same lowering (`lower.from_design` with `lower.LowerOptions`); every project file it writes keeps its bytes, and only `.fenolite/board.json` changes: it holds the footprint graphics and corner ratios that were written (the ten `ALTIUM` pins of the pinned builds moved for that reason alone). RT-A2 and RT-A3 compare the footprint graphics and the corner ratio; a project built before this change is skipped with `model-predates-graphics`. No KiCad output changes: a design read from KiCad holds the fields only after `backends.kicad.fpitems.with_footprint_items`. A model document of 0.2.0 reads as it is; **0.2.0 cannot read a model document that carries the new keys** (`graphics` and `texts` of a footprint, `corner_ratio` of a pad). `lens.altium.pcb_document` and `lens.altium.corner_ratios` are removed.

### Changed

- **The default output of an Altium build changes** (c0086): the Altium schematic draws each symbol's own graphics, follows the module tree at any depth, and carries port directions, buses, parameters and text outside ASCII. `--altium-symbols generic` gives the former output (one rectangle per part), byte for byte; `graphics` is the default by the maintainer's decision of 2026-10-06. New options `--altium-symbols generic|graphics` and `--altium-directions on|off`, new `result.schematic`, new info `altium.bus-flattened`. With `--altium-sheets modules` a nested module now gets its own sheet `<name>_<module path with ".">.SchDoc` instead of being drawn on the sheet of its top-level module; `flat` stays the default. Symbols and footprints from the catalog, and symbols the script authored, now build for `--target altium`. The properties of a part become hidden parameters. In the binary form a comment or a parameter value may hold Windows-1252 characters. The committed samples `blink`, `kicad_example`, `no_connect` and `routed` were regenerated; their earlier bytes, which the author reports covered, are kept under `tests/data/altium/generic/`. Nothing of this has been opened in Altium yet (Part Y of `docs/evidence/altium-schematic.md`); KiCad's importer reads the written library graphics as the model holds them.
- **`fenolite check` on Altium input runs two more stages by default, and can exit 5 where it exited 0** (c0088): `copper.clearance` (shorts, clearance and zone overlaps of the PCB document, with the Clearance rules the document holds) and `parity` (the PCB document against its schematic documents: references, values, footprints and nets), both without any tool. `--stages` selects the former set. The stage says what it could not judge and then carries `UNVERIFIED`: unpoured polygons, internal planes, Clearance rules outside the rule table, and filled zones that no clearance applies to (a pour is never judged against a default the document does not hold). `fenolite parity` takes an Altium project, project folder or PCB document. `fenolite build --target altium` judges the copper of the PCB document it is about to write and refuses a board with a short (exit 5, nothing written); `--copper-check warn` writes it, and the option is no usage error with that target any more. The result of an Altium build gains `copper_check`. The copper check judges no board-edge clearance on either backend. For Altium input only, each clearance rule is judged 5 nm lower, because a distance of an Altium document can read up to 4 nm closer after the unit conversion; KiCad boards are judged exactly as in 0.2.0.
- **`fenolite check` on an Altium PCB document can judge clearance where it judged shorts only, and exit 5 where it exited 0** (c0125): the Altium rule table reads more forms of a Clearance record. A blank object matrix and one whose every entry equals the rule's value are one clearance; the records of a clearance matrix between all net classes (`ISMATRIX` and the cell keys) are read; `ExistsOnLayer` of every copper layer of the board on both scopes gives a clearance rule with `layers`, and `OnMid` on a board without an internal signal layer applies to nothing (new reason `no-layer`, which is no unread rule). A matrix of differing clearances stays unmapped and its reason now counts the differing entries instead of quoting the matrix; the option that ignores the pads of one footprint, a layer condition for some of the copper layers and every other scope function stay unmapped too. The forms are read, never written. **On the public corpus this lifts ONE of the three PCB documents that had no clearance in force**, to the level `INFERRED`: the one whose records are a clearance matrix on a two-layer board, the only forms a neutral rule says exactly. The two others stay `UNVERIFIED` (`docs/evidence/altium-roundtrip.md`, "Light DRC over the corpus"). `map_rules`, `rulemap.lift` and `import_rules` take the board's copper layers (`layers=`); KiCad boards and every written file are unchanged.
- **The Altium import reads a Clearance matrix cell by cell, and the stage summary of a document check has one more key** (c0130): a Clearance record whose object matrix holds differing clearances gives its generic rule and one more clearance rule per cell (`<rule>/<kind>-<kind>`, with `item_kind` selectors for `track`, `pad`, `via` and `zone`), when the matrix is exact in those kinds; a matrix that tells an arc from a track or a through-hole pad from a surface pad stays unmapped and its reason names the pairs. Cells for fills, regions, text and holes are kept in the rule's bag. `summary.clearance_cells` (`judged`, `unjudged`) counts them, from the new `DesignRules.clearance_cells`. **On the public corpus this lifts one record, on the heavy document, which stays `UNVERIFIED`; the two other documents with such a matrix are not moved** (their matrices are not exact, and they carry the option that ignores the pads of one footprint). The measurement of that heavy document also found 28 false shorts of the import (vias without a pad on inner layers), listed on `docs/evidence/altium-roundtrip.md`. Read only; KiCad boards and every written file are unchanged.
- **The canonical model of an imported Altium project changes when a footprint model lists several pads for a pin** (c0123): the pads are pairs of `pin_pad_map`, no longer `pin_pads` entries of the extension bag, and `altium.import.pin-map` counts only a record without a pad and a pad that another pin holds. On the public project with such records the assignment comparison covers four more elements.
- **The output job that `fenolite build --target altium` writes now holds the Gerber settings, and every written `.OutJob` differs from the one an earlier build wrote** (c0138). No release wrote an output job (0.2.x writes none; the job is unreleased v0.3 work), so this concerns folders built from the development branch. **A rebuild into such a folder replaces the job and keeps the old one as `<name>.OutJob.bak`**, as it does for every other file the build wrote; it is refused instead (exit 7, `FEN-7001`, nothing written) only when the job was edited since that build or when the folder's `.fenolite/` record is gone, and then `--discard-layout` replaces it, again with the backup. **A rebuild of a folder built by 0.2.x adds the output job to the project file, keeping `<name>.PrjPcb.bak`**: a project file that is still as a build wrote it and does not list the job is written again with the job listed, so that Altium's Projects panel shows it; a project file that was changed since (Altium saved it, or you edited it), or that the folder's `.fenolite/` record does not know, is kept as before, whatever `--discard-layout`, with the info `altium.outjob-not-listed` and how to list the job. Why: run in Altium Designer 26, the job as written before produced drill, pick-and-place, bill-of-materials and print files and no Gerber layer file, because a Gerber output without a settings record plots nothing. The Gerber output now carries the complete record of 44 fields that the public saved jobs hold: millimetres, the decimals of the export preset's `gerbers.precision` (4 without a preset), and as plotted layers the layers of the board (silkscreen, paste, solder mask, the copper layers from top to bottom with a plane at its place, Mechanical 13 to 16). Every output, of every kind, gains the key `OutputDefault<i>=0`; no other output kind gains a settings record. **No board outline is plotted yet: the Gerber set of the written job holds no outline file**, because the PCB document holds the outline as the board shape and on no layer; `result.outjob.gerber.outline` says so, and you turn the outline on in the Gerber setup in Altium. `result.outjob` gains `gerber` (`unit`, `decimals`, `layers`, `outline`) and `result.outjob.defaults` no longer lists `gerbers.precision`. `outjob.from_preset` takes the board's copper stack (`copper=`), `write_outjob` refuses a Gerber output without the whole record, and `read_outjob` gives the settings of an output (`JobOutput.settings`, `record_fields`). Altium has not opened such a job yet: every row is `INFERRED` until Part O of the second Altium session is reported; the committed sample `tests/data/altium/outjob/blink.OutJob` changes (eight more lines).
- **The notes about a kept Altium project file name only what the file lacks** (c0138). A project file that exists in `--out` and is kept is now read with Fenolite's own project reader: `altium.pcb-not-in-project`, `altium.schlib-not-in-project`, `altium.sheets-not-in-project` and `altium.outjob-not-listed` are given only for documents the kept file really does not list, and each names those documents alone. Before, the first three were given on every rebuild into a folder with a project file, even one that listed everything. A kept file that cannot be read gets them as before, with a hint that says so.
- **`.fenolite/build.json` of an Altium build keeps a kept project file that is still as a build wrote it** (c0138). A rebuild that keeps `<name>.PrjPcb` (nothing has to change in it, or the output job is off) records the file again when its bytes are the ones the folder's record held, so that a later build still knows it as built and can add the output job to it. Before, a kept project file dropped out of the record at the first rebuild. A project file that is kept because it was changed, or that had no record, is not recorded. The record's file list is no list of what a run wrote: `result.files` and the receipt are, and they do not name a kept file.
- **An Altium board with internal planes is imported with fewer tracks.** A plane layer is stored in negative, so a line, an arc, a fill, a region or a text drawn on it cuts the plane and is no copper; the import made a track without a net of each such line. It now makes no entity of them, with or without a net: `altium.import.unmapped` counts them as `plane-cuts`, and the layer of the plane holds the count in its `altium` bag (`plane_cuts`). The ids of the other imported items do not change. The copper check on Altium input no longer filters those lines out of its view and reports the planes as before (`copper.item-unsupported` at `plane`); `equivalent --level 5` no longer counts them as copper on no net, and a rewrite of such a board (RT-A3) no longer writes them as tracks. On the two public documents with planes the import holds 74 and 43 tracks fewer, the number KiCad's own import of them holds (c0124).
- The Altium import keeps the holes of a poured region: copper inside a hole of a pour is no longer joined to it. A zone fill of an imported document is one ring that holds the outline and its holes, the form of a KiCad filled polygon (new `geometry.keyhole_ring`), so the copper check no longer reports tracks and vias in a hole as shorts and `equivalent --level 5` finds the islands of a pour. A hole outside its outline is dropped and reported by the new warning `altium.import.zone-hole-outside`. Imported boards whose pours have holes get other fill polygons than before; boards without holes are unchanged (c0122).
- **The counts of `fenolite roundtrip --level rta3` and of the stage `roundtrip.rta3` change on a document that holds vias whose drill equals their diameter** (c0128): such vias are now written by the trip and leave `unwritten.via`; no verdict changes. `AltiumBackend().write(design, rewrite=True)` (also `lower.write_design` and `lower.from_design`) is the caller's statement that the design is the reading of an Altium document and that the write gives it back; a via whose drill equals its diameter, which a saved document can hold, is then written as it was read. Without the argument nothing changes: `fenolite build --target altium`, `write_model` and a plain `write` refuse or leave out such a via as before, the committed samples keep their bytes, and `rewrite=True` is refused for a board that was not read from an Altium document. On the public document with 48 such vias the rewrite holds all 242 vias, and KiCad's importer reads it as Fenolite does.
- **The content of an imported Altium board with arcs changes** (c0127): an arc that is read from an arc record keeps the record's own centre, radius and angles in its `altium` bag (the pair `arc`, on an `Arc` and on a graphic of kind `arc`), and the write of a model (`AltiumBackend().write`, RT-A3) gives that record back while it still says the arc's three points within 2 nm. No id changes; a design read from KiCad never holds the pair; a build from a script writes the same bytes. An arc that was moved in the model is written from its points, as before, and an arc with a kept record is no longer refused when its three points lie on one line. On the heavy public document 7 of 517 arcs came back more than 2 nm away and 12 were not written; all 529 are now written and equal, RT-A3 holds on the eight public PCB documents, and `H-A-VER-RTA3` is `CORPUS-VERIFIED`. That level means that Fenolite reads its own rewrite of a public Altium PCB document back to an equal model inside the written scope, and that KiCad's importer reads the rewrite as Fenolite does at levels 1 to 5; it says nothing about Altium opening a written file (that stays `INFERRED` until the kit run of c0091 and c0092), and the scope leaves out footprint graphics (until c0126), component bodies (until c0121) and the items counted as not written in `docs/evidence/altium-roundtrip.md`. The stage `roundtrip.rta3` still reports `INFERRED`, no round-trip cell of the matrix is set, and the write stays experimental.
- The slack of the copper check on Altium input is a stated rule (c0131): one file unit (1/10 000 mil, 2.54 nm) per item of the pair, 5.08 nm for a pair, held as 5 whole nanometres and never rounded up. **No finding changes: the rule gives the 5 nm the check already used**, for Altium input only; KiCad boards are judged strictly at the nanometre, as in 0.2.0. The derivation (how far the conversion to nanometres moves a track, a via, a pad, a pour and an arc) is in `docs/formats/altium/import.md`. On the eight public PCB documents the slack removes 6 075 findings, all 1 to 4 nm short; 25 findings that are 8 to 20 nm short stay errors, and nothing is allow-listed. The rule is slightly tighter than the worst case of the conversion for a pad against a rectangular pad (5.55 to 6.36 nm against 5.08 nm) and is not widened, for lack of a public pair that shows it.
- **The JSON result of every `fenolite build --target altium` gains the key `pcb`** (c0121, repairing c0085 to its own spec): `result.pcb` holds `written`, `not_lowered` and the new `bodies`, after `copper`, and is `null` without a PCB document. The contract described the key since c0085, but only the library's build summary held it and the command never returned it. No written file changes. An Altium build writes the extruded component bodies of a board on request (`--altium-bodies extruded`, off by default); bodies that name a 3D model are reported. With the option, each extruded body of a footprint that has an outline and a height above its standoff becomes one record in both body storages of the PCB document, owned by its component, and the bodies of a footprint definition go into the PCB library; the write of a model takes the same choice (`bodies="extruded"` on `AltiumBackend.write`, `lens.altium.write_model` and `AltiumBackend.model_roundtrip`), and RT-A2 and RT-A3 then compare the bodies that were written. Without the option every file of a build is the file of before, byte for byte, and no body is ever made for a footprint that has none. **Experimental, and the evidence is thin**: the keys of the record were measured on 1272 saved bodies of five public documents of three repositories, 1265 of them from one repository; two keys (`MODELID`, `MODEL.CHECKSUM`) have no known rule and hold stand-ins; and no written body has been opened in Altium, so the option stays off until step X8 of the author report is in. New sample `tests/data/altium/body2/`.
- **Catalog symbols show a pin name only where it can be read, so some symbols look different** (c0134). The transistors, thyristors and photodetectors, `Schottky_Diode`, `Potentiometer`, `Transformer`, `Switch_SPDT`, `Pushbutton_NO`, `Relay_SPDT`, `Dual_LED_Common_Cathode`, `Optocoupler`, `Bridge_Rectifier`, `Operational_Amplifier` and `Comparator` no longer draw their pin names, which lay on each other and on the drawing in KiCad and in Altium; the amplifiers mark their two inputs with a plus and a minus stroke, the bridge its four terminals, the optocoupler its emitter. `Linear_Regulator`, `Offline_Power_Controller`, `Microcontroller` and `Power_Module` keep their names in a larger box, `Schottky_Diode` has the longer pins of `Diode`, and `Capacitor_Polarized` and `Capacitor_Electrolytic` carry their plus mark below the lead, clear of the pin number. In the example library `FenoliteDemo.kicad_sym`, `CONN2` no longer shows its pin numbers a second time as names, `MCU8` and `DUAL_OPAMP` are larger, `MCU8` has longer pins further apart with its names clear of its clock wedge and its numbers clear of its inversion bubbles, and `R_V` has longer pins. A design that uses one of these symbols gets a different schematic on its next build, once: the symbol's look, and for the enlarged ones the places of its pins. Pin numbers, pin names, nets, designators and the board do not change. A test now measures the rule on every symbol Fenolite authors, with the marks KiCad draws for inverted and clock pins, at KiCad's text size and at an estimate of Altium's that nobody has measured in Altium yet.
- **The two LED lands of the catalog are documented as cathode-first** (c0144): `LED0603_Kingbright_APT1608SURCK` and `LED0805_Kingbright_APT2012SURCK` number their pads as the manufacturer does, pad 1 the cathode, while the symbol `Fenolite:LED` has pin 1 anode. `docs/catalog/sources.md` and `docs/catalog/coverage.md` now say, as they do for `SOD128_Nexperia_CFP5`, that the pair needs `pad_map={"1": "2", "2": "1"}`; without it the anode's net lies on the cathode's pad. Check a design of 0.2.x that uses one of these lands: nothing in a build changes, the footprints and their summaries are the same. The four kit samples that used the pair now carry the map and connect the LED by pin name, so their schematics draw it the right way round and their boards keep every pad's net.

- A component read from an Altium PCB document takes the designator text the board shows as its reference, and its source designator only without one, so the components of a repeated sheet no longer share a reference in a board read on its own; the fault was found by the equivalence triangle (c0045).
- `fenolite bom` no longer puts a part marked "do not populate" (DNP) on the line of fitted parts of the same value: with `exclude_dnp = false` in the column template, a line is all DNP or all fitted, whether or not `group_by` names `dnp`, for both sources, so no quantity counts a part that is not fitted. A DNP line stands where its first reference puts it, like every line. With `--against`, the `key` of a DNP line ends with `DNP` (c0094; guide `docs/assembly.md`).

### Fixed

- Windows: `kicad-cli.exe` is found on `PATH` and in KiCad's default install folders, which KiCad's installer does not put on `PATH`; files Fenolite writes keep LF line ends; a board on another drive than the working folder can be routed; a drawing-sheet path starting with `/` counts as absolute.
- `fenolite route` reads the net classes from the project file beside the board. Before, every net was routed with the default track width, clearance and via size, whatever its class said: a KiCad board holds no net class.
- `fenolite route --router freerouting` lists a net under `unrouted` when Freerouting reports that it left one of its connections open. Before, a net with any copper counted as routed, so a partly routed net was reported as done; KiCad's DRC in `fenolite check` still found it.
- A board whose rotated pad or rotated footprint carries a slotted hole is read and written again, and its footprints can be written as library files: the writer refused them (`KiCad writer cannot represent this slot rotation`, `KiCad oval drills can encode only horizontal or vertical slot axes`). `Padstack.hole_rotation` is now the slot's axis in the pad's own frame everywhere; on a board it used to include the pad's angle.
- Geometry: the documented and tested bound of two successive `Transform.apply` calls is `(1 + |cos θ| + |sin θ|)/2` nm per axis, at most `(1 + √2)/2 ≈ 1.2071` nm, not 1 nm; `test_composition_rounds_once` no longer fails at random, and a test reaches the bound exactly. No code changes (c0050).
- Script copper in the Altium build (change c0053): `fenolite build --target altium` now writes the tracks, vias and stitching vias that the design script declares (`Design.track`, `Design.via`, `Design.stitch`) into the experimental `<name>.PcbDoc`; before, they were left out without an issue. An intent that does not resolve refuses the build, an intent that creates nothing is reported, and with `--copper-from` the board wins with one `altium.not-lowered` info.
- `fenolite check` no longer reports `kicad.drc.rules-not-loaded` for rules that were loaded on a board with 499 or more clearance violations: `kicad-cli` stops reporting clearance violations near 499 per run and can leave the canary's own out, so such a run is now `inconclusive` with the reason `clearance-limit` (`kicad.drc.rules-unchecked`). The two-run canary test compares two DRC runs against what was measured to repeat, and the contract says which part of `check`'s output repeats between runs (c0051).
- Altium PCB document in the form Altium saves: `<name>.PcbDoc` now holds the whole `Board6` record (board keys, the outline as a polygon, sheet, sub-stack, drill pair, routing keys and snap grid, written by rule in `docboard.py` on top of `libboard.py`), net and component records with the common keys and unique ids, `UniqueIDPrimitiveInformation`, the option storages (`Advanced Placer Options6`, `Pin Swap Options6`, `Design Rule Checker Options6`, `PadViaLibrary`, `PadViaLibraryCache`, `LayerKindMapping`, `ConstraintManager`, `SignalClasses`) and 13 more empty storages; `write_pcbdoc` takes the file name. The first form gave a "catastrophic" error in Altium Designer 26.5 (maintainer's report, c0035); the new one opens there and its change order from the schematic reports no difference (author report of 2026-10-03). Texts, pads, tracks and arcs keep their short records.
- Altium PCB library opens in Altium Designer's form: `<name>.PcbLib` now holds the 53-byte `FileHeader` (version and library id), the whole board record in `Library/Data` (layer stack, grid, layer sets and view keys, written by rule in `libboard.py`) and the `Library` side streams (`EmbeddedFonts`, `ModelsNoEmbed`, `Textures`, `ComponentParamsTOC`, `PadViaLibrary`); `FileHeaderSix` of `<name>.PcbDoc` carries its id. The first form gave a "catastrophic" error in Altium Designer (maintainer's report, c0035); the new one opens in Altium Designer 26.5 (author report of 2026-10-03).
